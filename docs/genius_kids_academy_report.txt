Executive Summary
This document is the full technical and functional specification for the Genius Kids
Academy ERP — a custom web application that will replace the current Excel and paperbased system across all 13 branches.
The system covers two branch types: academies (workshops, levels, evaluation,
progression) and kindergartens (age groups, daily logs, monthly reports, communication
books). It manages the full lifecycle of students and staff, tracks every dinar that enters or
leaves each branch, and gives parents a live window into their child's attendance and
progress.
Scope in numbers: ~13 branches · ~5,200 students · 2 branch types · 9 functional modules
· 1 shared PostgreSQL database · 3 years of historical data to migrate.
What it replaces: one Excel workbook per branch (student lists, payment tracking,
timetables, expense logs) and paper books (Soroban follow-up books, kindergarten
communication books, class journals).
What it adds that Excel cannot do: real-time absence alerts to parents, duplicatestudent prevention across branches, a cash balance that is always current, one login for a
parent with children in several branches, and a full audit trail of every financial change.

Business Context
Genius Kids Academy is a private school group in Jijel, Algeria, with 13 branches serving
children aged 3 to 14. Each branch is either a kindergarten or an academy, and people
(students and staff) can move between branches.
Academy model. The academy teaches workshops (Soroban mental arithmetic, robotics,
French, English, AI, Rubik's cube, Quran, horse riding, swimming, support lessons, a
summer club and competitions). A workshop has one or more courses; a course is a level
and has one book. Students move through books in strict order, cannot skip a book,
cannot take two books of the same workshop at once, and repeat a book if they fail its

tests. Each workshop defines its own way of evaluating: Soroban uses timed operations
and three tests with an 80/100 pass mark; some groups are tested orally with right/wrong
answers; robotics uses projects; horse riding uses challenges instead of a book.
Kindergarten model. Children are split by age, then into groups of the same age. Each
child follows a program, owns a numbered book whose content depends on age and level,
and gets a daily report rated A/B/C (good, average, weak) on meals, sleep, interaction,
crying and clothes. Teachers also write a monthly report that administration approves
before parents can see it. An orthophonist can leave notes, and parents can send simple
notes that staff answer.
Money. Payment is cash only. Academy fees are paid per level, upfront or in installments.
Kindergarten fees are paid monthly or yearly, and yearly subscribers get a discount.
Registration is paid every year. The receptionist can set the price per customer, so
discounts are dynamic. Payroll is handled outside the system and is out of scope.
Who uses it: administration, receptionists, teachers and nannies, specialists, other staff
(cleaning, maintenance, cooks), students (including under-14s, who log in themselves)
and parents.

Technical Architecture
The proposed stack keeps everything in one Django application: staff and parents reach it
through the web, and it alone talks to the database, the file store and the notification
queue.

Database Design
The first-pass schema is written ( school_erp_schema_v1.sql ): about 75 tables, 3 views
and seed data, loaded and tested on PostgreSQL 16 with fake data. It is the specification
the developers translate into Django models.
Conventions. Table names carry a module prefix ( core_ , org_ , iam_ , hr_ , acad_ , kg_ ,
comm_ , fin_ , mig_ ) so they map directly to Django apps. Primary keys are bigint

identities, money is NUMERIC(12,2) in dinars, timestamps are timestamptz , and
weekdays follow ISO numbering (1 = Monday).

Rules the database itself enforces
These were tested and fail correctly when broken:
A level cannot be started unless the previous level was passed, or an admin records a
placement note.
A student has only one open book per workshop.

A room or a trainer cannot be double-booked, in the weekly timetable or on a specific
date.
A group cannot use a room from another branch.
An installment cannot be overpaid, and a payment cannot be allocated beyond its
amount.
Payments cannot be deleted, only voided with a reason.
A price different from the list price needs a reason and the name of the person who
set it.
The person who reviews a test result must differ from the one who corrected it; the
same rule applies to kindergarten monthly reports.
Only one academic year can be current, and years cannot overlap.

Derived, never stored
Cash balances, daily income and expense summaries, and invoice remaining balances are
computed by views from payments and cash entries, so they cannot drift out of sync.

Functional Requirements by Module
Each requirement below comes from the requirements interviews, the Excel files or the
paper books. Items marked (to confirm) are my reading of a photo or an assumption.

1. Identity and access
Anyone can create an account online; it stays pending until the required fees are paid,
then it is activated. This applies to both the parent and the student.
Students under 14 log in themselves; parents log in separately and see their children.
Admins create roles by picking permissions from a fixed catalogue, and assign roles
per person and per branch (or all branches). One person can hold several roles.
Every sensitive action (grade change, price override, payment void, medical data
view) is written to the audit log.

2. Organization
Manage 13 branches, each a kindergarten or an academy, with rooms, room capacity
and equipment per room.
Academic years start at the beginning of the school year; a late student can enroll if a
place is available.

3. People and family
Student data: Arabic and Latin names, birth date, contact details, school grade,
previous schools, medical information, special needs, documents and photos.
Link each student to father, mother or guardian, with phone numbers, emergency
contact and custody flags.
Parents set in the app who may pick up the child, either a person already in the
system or a one-off person. Reception checks the ID at the desk. No pickup log is
kept.

4. Staff and HR
Staff profiles with job titles (teaching, administrative, cleaning, maintenance, cooks,
orthophonist and others) and assignments that can change between branches.
Staff attendance is marked once a day by administration; a fingerprint device can feed
the same table later.
Staff apply online and are invited by email for an interview; the application status is
tracked.

5. Academy
Define workshops, their courses (levels) in order, the book for each course and a pass
rule per course (for Soroban: 3 tests, 80 out of 100 each).
Create groups per branch and year with trainer and capacity; build the weekly
timetable by group size and room capacity. Hours are mostly fixed, rooms may change
for a session.
Mark attendance per session. Administration calls parents about absences and
lateness, and records justifications received by phone.
An absence creates a make-up session owed to the student. A failed test creates a
make-up session for that test only.
Evaluations are dynamic per workshop: timed operations, oral right/wrong, projects,
challenge milestones, scores. The teacher corrects and administration reviews before
the result is published.
A student who passes all required tests moves to the next book; a student who fails
repeats the book.
Students download online books and homework only for the courses they are enrolled
in.

6. Kindergarten
Group children by age band, then into groups of the same age; each child has a
numbered book whose content follows age and level.
Daily attendance per child.
Daily log rated A, B or C on a fixed set of items: breakfast, lunch, snack, sleep, study,
interaction, crying, clothes change, plus a free note (to confirm items).
Monthly report with the same items and a note; administration approves before
parents see it.

Incident log for behavior, sleep, eating and learning problems (to confirm categories).
Orthophonist notes, visible to staff or also to parents.
Child information page filled by parents (food, sleep, clothing, illness, temperament)
and a weekly rotating menu shown to parents.

7. Communication
Parents send a simple note per child to administration or the teacher; staff answer
once. Administration switches this feature on or off, or limits it to time windows.
Notifications go out by email, SMS, WhatsApp and Telegram through one queue:
absence alerts, payment receipts, report approval, interview invitations.

8. Finance
Price plans per branch, workshop, level and year; kindergarten plans for monthly and
yearly billing; registration billed yearly.
Invoices with lines, an installment schedule (one installment for upfront payment,
several for split payment) and PDF output.
Cash payments produce numbered receipts per branch and year without gaps; a
payment can be voided but never deleted.
One cash box per branch records expenses by category and handovers of cash to the
owner or head office.
The daily summary shows income, expenses, handovers and the running balance for
each branch.

9. Data migration
Load the Excel history into staging, review rows the importer could not match, then
convert them to real records with a link back to the source sheet and row.

Non-Functional Requirements
The load is small (about 5,200 students across 13 branches), so the priorities are
correctness, privacy and safe backups rather than raw performance.

Migration Plan
Everything that exists in Excel, including grades where they exist, will be loaded into the
new database. At least three years of history are involved. The team does not yet have the
real data files, so the plan is designed around the structure of the sample files received.
1. Collect. Obtain the real workbooks for every branch, the list of all 13 branches with
their type, and any file holding grades or Soroban test times. None of these are
available yet.
2. Stage. Load each sheet row as raw data into mig_import_row , one batch per file and
sheet.
3. Clean and match. Normalize names, split family and given names, unify level and
group labels, and detect people who appear in several sheets or branches.
4. Review. Staff review the rows flagged as unmatched or ambiguous before anything
becomes a real record.
5. Convert. Create people, enrollments, invoices, installments and payments, keeping a
link to the source file, sheet and row in mig_legacy_ref .
6. Reconcile. Compare totals per branch and per month against the Excel cash sheets
before go-live.

Data problems already seen in the samples
Names are written as one field and the order of family name and given name is
inconsistent, so splitting needs a review queue.
Level and group labels are inconsistent (for example p.section 2 and p section
2 ).

Some hidden sheets contain #REF! errors and likely obsolete prices.
A January date in the kindergarten expense sheet is in 2025 while the neighboring
months are in 2026.
The sample student and payment rows are mostly blank, so real data quality is
unknown.

Risks
Payment history is the hardest part to trust: receipt numbers, installment dates and
partial payments are typed by hand.
Grades kept on paper cannot be migrated automatically; the team must decide
whether to key them in or archive scans as files.
